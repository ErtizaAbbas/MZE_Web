Why broker friction belongs in every backtest

EAErtiza Abbas12 min read

Most strategies look brilliant in a backtest. Far fewer survive contact with a real brokerage account. The gap is rarely the signal itself; it is everything that happens between the signal and the fill.

The three hidden costs

Every trade pays some combination of three frictions. Leave any of them out and the equity curve is fiction.

  • Spread: the difference between the bid and the ask, paid on entry and exit.
  • Commission: explicit broker and exchange fees, fixed or proportional.
  • Slippage: the difference between the price you expected and the price you got.
A strategy that trades often and earns a little per trade is the one most exposed to friction.

Modelling it honestly

The simplest robust approach is to charge a cost per round trip that reflects the instrument and the market conditions you actually trade in, then check that the result still holds out of sample.

Net return = Gross return − (spread + commission + slippage)

From there, stress-test: double the costs and see whether the edge survives. If it disappears, it was never an edge.

Key takeaways

  • Always include spread, commission and slippage.
  • Validate out of sample, and stress-test costs.
  • Prefer fewer, higher-quality trades when frictions are high.
